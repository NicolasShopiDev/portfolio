The choice between a percentage and a fixed price looks minor and it decides how much maintenance the store costs you every season.

## Percentage off retail

A discount applied on top of the retail price. When you raise B2C prices, the wholesale ones move with them. This is what I recommend by default: no list goes stale.

The problem shows up when margin is not uniform. If wholesale pays 40% less in one product family and 25% less in another, a single global percentage does not cover it and you either split the catalog or move to fixed prices.

## Fixed price per variant

The price list written by hand, SKU by SKU. It gives total control and it has a cost: every B2C price change has to be replicated here, and if it is not, the wholesale buyer ends up paying more than the retail customer. I have seen that in production more than once.

My rule: percentage by default, fixed price only on the SKUs where the agreement with the customer forces it, and a price review on the calendar. If you mix them, write down somewhere which SKUs are fixed, because inside the admin they do not stand out at a glance.

## What a price list is not

It is not the way to give every account its own price without Plus: catalogs are assigned per market, so the list reaches every company in that market. Per-account pricing needs [Shopify Plus](/en/glossary/shopify-plus/) or a public app.

Detail in the [B2B pricing documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/catalogs).
