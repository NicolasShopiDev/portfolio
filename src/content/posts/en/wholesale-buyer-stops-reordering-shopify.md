To identify wholesale buyers who stop reordering, compare their last valid purchase with their buying rhythm and commercial situation. Start with a review list for your team, rather than an automatic email assuming the customer has left.

“They haven't ordered in a while.”

Fair enough... a while for whom? Weekly replenishment and seasonal purchasing run on different clocks.

The first task is giving that silence some meaning.

## Define whom you are following

The company, a location or the person behind the last purchase? Combining a chain's branches can hide an inactive location. Treating each email independently can make a change of buyer resemble a lost account.

Shopify structures [B2B access and history](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/customer-login-and-accounts) around accounts associated with company locations. Use that distinction to define your review unit; email counts alone do not describe commercial activity.

Hypothetical example: a distributor supplies two shops. One orders regularly; the other prepares seasonal campaigns. Explain what you are comparing before assigning identical alerts.

## Decide what counts as a valid purchase

A pending quote is not equivalent to accepted replenishment. A cancellation should not automatically count as sufficient activity to mark a customer recovered either.

Write down which records qualify and which require separate review. If orders arrive outside Shopify, decide how to recognise them before labelling somebody inactive.

This is a proposed business criterion, not a claim that a native report automatically combines all your channels. For requests not yet accepted, consult the [B2B order review guide](/en/blog/shopify-b2b-order-awaiting-review/).

## Treat rhythm as a clue, not a verdict

In the fictional example, one shop replenishes consumables regularly and another prepares a seasonal selection. The same last-order date can call for different decisions.

Do not invent a thirty-day threshold because it looks tidy on a dashboard. Review available history with the commercial owner. Where purchases are sparse, acknowledge uncertainty: you can still review the account without claiming a pattern the evidence cannot support.

And for heaven’s sake, do not send “we miss you” to somebody who has spent two weeks waiting for your quote. What they miss is a reply.

## Separate detection from contact

I suggest that the first version produces an internal list. Each entry should explain why it appears, what needs checking and who decides the next step.

| Proposed field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Company and location | Avoid mixing purchasing units |
| Last qualifying purchase | Make the criterion reviewable |
| Expected rhythm and basis | Separate evidence from intuition |
| Pending situation | Quote, issue or purchase elsewhere |
| Owner and action | Review, wait or prepare a conversation |

This is a suggested diagnostic tool. No real list or reminder was configured for this article.

After reviewing a sample, discuss false positives: which accounts appeared without needing recovery? That conversation improves the criterion before you multiply inappropriate messages.

## Check how difficult buying again actually is

Spotting silence achieves little if buyers must reconstruct a purchase by email. Official help documents reordering from B2B history. That is not a subscription or a promise that old orders retain all their original conditions.

For a controlled case, I suggest checking whether buyers find references, understand current conditions and complete the journey. An available feature need not be well integrated into your portal.

For a new selection by reference, consult the [wholesale quick-order guide](/en/blog/sku-quick-order-shopify-b2b/). This article addresses a different need: recognising when to review a recurring relationship and making its next purchase easier.

To assess a [Shopify B2B portal](/en/shopify-b2b-developer/), bring three anonymised examples: a regular buyer, a seasonal buyer and one awaiting a decision. More useful than “we need AI customer recovery”.
