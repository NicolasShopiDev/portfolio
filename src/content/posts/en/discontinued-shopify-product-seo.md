When a product stops selling, decide what its visitors need before archiving, deleting or redirecting it. Removing a commercial reference does not, by itself, answer what should happen to its URL.

The supplier says it is never coming back.

Fine... except the links still sending people to that page as though nothing happened.

Those visitors deserve a better response than a shrug.

## Confirm what has actually been retired

Is it the entire product, one size or a seasonal model? Is there a genuine replacement? Do buyers still need dimensions, compatibility information or instructions?

“Unavailable this week” and “no longer manufactured” are different problems. The first concerns supply. The second might require changing the purpose of a page.

If Shopify says sold out despite available units, start with the [availability diagnosis](/en/blog/shopify-sold-out-with-stock/). Here, commercial retirement is already confirmed; we are not correcting inventory quantities.

## Archiving organises the catalog; it does not design the next visit

Shopify’s [status and availability documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-status) explains that archived products are hidden from the online store. Unlisted is not a shortcut to preserving search visibility either: it adds noindex and removes the product from the sitemap while retaining a direct URL.

“Archive it to preserve SEO” therefore needs rather more explanation. An admin decision cannot replace checking what a visitor receives.

Before changing anything, open the address people use and record what they should find afterwards. Where several people maintain the catalog, write that decision down. Somebody can correctly retire a reference while unintentionally breaking the journey another team expected to keep.

## Give a retained page a useful job

Hypothetical example: a shop retires a lamp, but existing customers still need to know which shade fits the old model. Preserving compatibility information and clearly explaining that the lamp is no longer sold could serve a purpose.

That requires assessing content, purchase behaviour and maintenance. Leaving a disabled button and two words on a page for five years is hardly a strategy.

Ask what somebody could accomplish there: confirm a measurement, locate a spare or choose an alternative. If you can help, there is a reason to assess an information page. This does not mean every theme or status change implements it automatically.

And for heaven’s sake, stop displaying “back soon” when you know it is not coming back. Nostalgia is not a replenishment policy.

## A replacement should actually replace something

Another fictional example: the new lamp uses a different mounting system. Sending somebody looking for the old model straight there, without explanation, could produce the wrong purchase.

Assess need, compatibility and differences before choosing a destination. “Same category” does not establish equivalence.

Shopify supports [URL redirects](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/menus-and-links/url-redirect). For a rule that fails to apply, consult the [redirect troubleshooting guide](/en/blog/shopify-redirect-not-working/). Here, the important decision comes first: justify where the visit should go.

## Sometimes there is no equivalent page

You do not need to invent an alternative for every item ever sold. Google documents how it processes [HTTP responses](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/troubleshooting/http-status-codes): a successful server response does not guarantee indexing, and an indexed URL returning 404 eventually leaves the index.

Consider what remains to solve for the buyer. Do not turn “avoid every 404” into an instruction to redirect everything home. Equally, keeping a product page available cannot promise preserved rankings. Observe the outcome rather than declaring it in advance.

## Make a small decision record

For a hypothetical sample, I would record the following before bulk retirement:

| Question | Decision to record |
| --- | --- |
| What did the visitor need? | Purchase, comparison or compatibility information |
| What remains useful? | Specific information worth retaining |
| Is there a replacement? | Destination and differences the buyer must understand |
| Who maintains the response? | Owner and review point |
| Which links need updating? | Affected menus, guides and owned campaigns |

Then check the old URL, final content and journey from your own links in the languages you sell in. This is a proposed protocol, not a completed audit or forecast.

Start with three references. If you cannot explain what the buyer should find, a bulk operation only multiplies the uncertainty. To assess [Shopify theme development](/en/services/custom-shopify-themes/), bring those decisions: they are much more useful than “please avoid losing SEO”.
