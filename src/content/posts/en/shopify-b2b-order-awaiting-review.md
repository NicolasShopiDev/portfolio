If a wholesale purchase arrives as a Shopify draft, check whether its company location submits orders for review. That can be the intended checkout outcome, rather than a missing order or abandonment.

The buyer thinks they finished. Sales expects something else. The warehouse is unsure whether to prepare.

The issue may not be a button... but an undefined meaning of “order received”.

## Check how checkout ended

Ask what the buyer saw and find the corresponding record. An email or absence from the expected list is insufficient to classify the case.

Shopify’s [B2B checkout settings](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/checkout-and-orders/checkout-settings) allow all purchases for a company location to become drafts for review. That initial checkout collects no payment.

Separate submission from acceptance. For a third-party wholesale app, establish its own journey rather than assuming native behaviour applies.

## Find the queue somebody must handle

The [B2B draft order guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/checkout-and-orders/draft-orders) locates these requests on the Drafts page and explains completion steps. Check the actual flow rather than guessing from notification names.

The [plan feature table](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/getting-started/plan-features) includes checkout-to-draft on Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus. This does not promise every customised approval rule: thresholds, integrations and extra tools require separate verification.

Decide who monitors that queue and what they need to act. A review without an owner is an order waiting for somebody to remember it.

## Define exactly what needs reviewing

Hypothetical example: a brand receives a campaign order. Sales must confirm a requested delivery date before accepting. Review has a specific purpose.

Now imagine every order requires review because somebody enabled it at launch and nobody discussed it. The same status can represent either a commercial decision or unnecessary waiting.

Complete this record before automating:

| Decision | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Reason | Which condition requires review? |
| Owner | Who can accept or request changes? |
| Information | What must that person check? |
| Communication | What does the buyer understand is pending? |
| Next step | What follows review? |

This is a proposed process, not a tested store workflow.

If nobody can explain the decision, more emails cannot resolve it. Clarify first; then choose what to automate.

## Acceptance, payment and preparation are separate checks

Do not assume payment because a buyer submitted their selection. Changing status just to place a record where another team expects it is also a poor shortcut.

Record commercial acceptance, actual payment state and preparation authorisation separately. Your procedure depends on terms and configuration; it should describe events rather than disguise the process.

For payment deadlines, consult the [B2B payment terms guide](/en/blog/payment-terms-shopify-b2b/). To hold goods during review, read the [inventory reservation guide](/en/blog/reserve-stock-shopify-draft-order/). One decision does not establish the others.

## Check what buyers receive too

A receipt message should explain what happened and what remains. “We are reviewing your request” differs from “your order is confirmed”.

For the campaign example, you could acknowledge the selection and explain that the requested date awaits confirmation. This is proposed wording, not a response-time promise or a claim about Shopify’s default notifications.

Buyers should not need to investigate your admin. Calling to ask whether they purchased anything suggests the portal needs clearer communication.

## Run a small test before changing the flow

I propose a test company and controlled basket. Record selected location, final action displayed, resulting record and received communication. Then follow the agreed review procedure.

Check that sales can act and the warehouse does not interpret a pending request as dispatch authorisation. For an ERP, verify the event it receives too. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not observed results.

The [wholesale account approval guide](/en/blog/wholesale-account-requests-shopify/) covers who can start buying. This article covers a purchase already submitted.

To assess a [Shopify B2B portal](/en/shopify-b2b-developer/) fitting your team, start with an anonymised case: what the buyer requested, what needed review and who should advance it. More useful than “we want order approval”.

Sources checked in November 2026. Example and protocol are hypothetical; no client orders or payments were processed or validated for this article.
