## The six questions, and what you are listening for

**1. Which live stores can I visit, and what exactly did you do on them?** Not screenshots: URLs. And the second half of the question matters more than the first, because several people have worked on any given store. A good answer sounds like "this order table and the location picker"; a bad one sounds like "we handle this account".

**2. Which part of this needs no apps and no code?** This is where you find out whether they know the platform or only their own trade. A lot of what gets sent to development in B2B is configuration: [quantity rules](/en/glossary/quantity-rules/), [volume pricing](/en/glossary/volume-pricing/), catalogs. Somebody billing you to build what already ships either does not know, or prefers not to.

**3. Which Shopify plan does this leave me on?** If what you are asking for needs [Plus](/en/glossary/shopify-plus/) and nobody mentions it, you will find out at the end. It happens most with custom Functions and with per-account pricing.

**4. How do you hand over the code, and where does it live?** The right answer includes a repository you have access to. If the only place the theme exists is your store's admin, your supplier is your backup.

**5. What happens when something breaks in three months?** Do not look for an eternal guarantee: look for the question not surprising them. Somebody who has maintained stores will talk about theme updates, API versions and which part stays theirs.

**6. Which of the things I asked for do you recommend I skip?** The most useful of the six. Somebody who says everything is a good idea will say yes to a fourth catalog you do not need and to a custom app that could have been a metafield.

## What I would answer

To the first: [twenty projects with their URL](/en/projects/), and each entry says what I did. To the fourth: a repository of yours from day one. And on the sixth, the answer I give most often on a first call is that order flow pays off more than changing plan.
