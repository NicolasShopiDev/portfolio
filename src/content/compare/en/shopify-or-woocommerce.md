## The right question is not technical

Both platforms sell online perfectly well. The difference that actually decides is **whose problem it is when something breaks**.

On Shopify, the checkout, the hosting, the scaling and payment compliance belong to Shopify. On WooCommerce they belong to you, and that is not a flaw: it is the price of total control. The mistake is choosing WooCommerce for the control and then having nobody to exercise it.

## Where WooCommerce still wins

- **When the store is a small part of a content site.** A publication, a community, or a project with hundreds of articles already in WordPress that sells something on the side.
- **When you need to change the buying process in a way Shopify does not allow.** [Shopify's checkout](/en/glossary/checkout-extensibility/) extends at specific points; it is not reordered or laid out freely.
- **When the data model is genuinely strange.** Products that are not products, calculations that are not prices.

## Where Shopify wins, which is the usual case

When the business is selling. There is no server to size for a campaign, the checkout has been tested by millions of orders, and what used to be a wholesale plugin is now [native B2B](/en/glossary/shopify-b2b/) on nearly every plan.

And there is one advantage that rarely makes the comparison tables: on Shopify, maintenance cost tends towards zero when nobody touches the store. On WooCommerce, an unmaintained store degrades by itself, because PHP, WordPress and the plugins keep moving.

## If you are considering migrating

The risk in a migration is not the data: it is the URLs. Traffic loss almost always comes from old addresses landing on a 404 or all redirected to the home page. The URL inventory comes first, before design is discussed, and it is the part you cannot cut.
