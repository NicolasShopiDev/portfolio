## What changed, and why the question is new

For years, selling wholesale on Shopify meant installing an app. Today Shopify's documentation lists B2B on Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus, and excludes it only from Starter and Retail. That turns the app, in most cases, into a layer you no longer need.

The technical difference has a very practical consequence: with native B2B, the [catalog](/en/glossary/b2b-catalog/) price is **already resolved** when the theme asks for it. With an app that calculates the discount in the cart, there is a window where the buyer sees one price and pays another, and that window is the source of half the support tickets on a wholesale portal.

## Where an app still wins

One case, and it is specific: **a negotiated price account by account without Shopify Plus**. Without Plus, catalogs are assigned to B2B markets rather than companies, so a per-account list leaves two routes: move to Plus or use a public app. If the plan upgrade is not in the budget, the app is the right answer.

The second case is about the calendar rather than the technology: if you need the portal live next week and the app brings the whole flow with its own design, it can work as a bridge. It is worth knowing it is a bridge.

## The mistake that costs money

Paying a monthly app fee expecting it to solve the order flow. Almost everything people ask a wholesale app for is not pricing: it is a per-variant [order table](/en/glossary/quick-order/) with stock, SKU search, quantity rules on the field itself, reordering the last order. That is front-end work on the theme, it works on any plan with B2B, and no pricing app brings it ready-made for your brand.

It is where I have seen the most budget wasted: a monthly fee covering 20% of the problem, with the other 80% still missing.
