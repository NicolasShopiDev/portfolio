## The real risk of each, said plainly

The freelancer risk is obvious: it is one person. If they disappear, the project loses its memory. That is mitigated with three specific things, and all three are yours by contract: the code in a repository that is **yours** from day one, changes in files of their own rather than scattered through the theme, and the decisions written down somewhere.

The agency risk is less visible: distance. You talk to whoever manages, not whoever writes, and on technical projects that introduces a translation step in every iteration. It also happens that the profile shown in the proposal is not the one who ends up touching your store.

## The hybrid case, which is the most common and nobody mentions

Plenty of brands work with an agency for design, content and campaigns, and with a specialist for the hard technical part. I work that way myself: since October 2024 I have been the sole front-end developer at a B2B-focused Shopify Plus agency whose client storefronts add up to over €36M in annual GMV, while also running direct projects.

It is the combination that works best when there is an agency that already knows the brand and a technical piece that needs specialisation: B2B, [Functions](/en/glossary/shopify-functions/), checkout or performance.

## How to decide, in one sentence

If you can write the scope on one page and the problem is technical, a freelancer. If the problem is "we need to grow and we do not know where to start", an agency. And if you already have an agency and what is missing is a specialism, do not change agency: add the specialist.

The [six questions worth asking before hiring](/en/questions/what-to-ask-a-shopify-developer/) apply equally to both.
